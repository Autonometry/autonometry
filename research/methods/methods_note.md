# Autonomy Meter: a validated method for measuring the human share of robot work

Methods note, v0.1 — 2026-10-30. Companion documents: [prior_art.md]({{artifact:40cbcdd3-0596-4291-99ef-c682a4596501}}), [definitions.md]({{artifact:2168860d-f130-48a2-a36a-2e52c2e5c3a7}}), [gaming.md]({{artifact:6f17f197-1c45-443f-9341-185efd6e5384}}), [trust.md]({{artifact:1a6dad1c-3cc4-4a97-8fca-0d91307c57db}}), [standard_v0.1.md]({{artifact:38f66165-3d49-4608-b10a-9c2dbd69e646}}), [event.schema.json]({{artifact:efd8879d-429a-495f-81c9-4d3404f57a9b}}), reference implementation [reference.zip]({{artifact:54785d39-c1b3-4a38-a036-bdf1833e2532}}) (autonomy_meter v0.1.1, [README]({{artifact:66d235f2-f87a-4adf-bb3f-241d1a02e96c}})).

Citation convention: every external claim is followed by a source id linked to the URL retrieved on 2026-10-30 and a tag — VERIFIED (primary text read), CLAIM (official scope/abstract page or secondary record only), UNKNOWN (could not be verified). The bibliography (103 sources) is Appendix A and `sources_all.csv`. Every number in Sections 5-7 was computed by the saved scripts and read back from their output tables.

## Abstract

Claims that a robot is "90 % autonomous" are currently unfalsifiable: no standard defines the control states to be logged, no regulation attaches an uncertainty statement to intervention counts, and the party being measured writes the log. Autonomy Meter provides (1) a seven-state, per-scope control taxonomy with binding edge-case rules; (2) six metrics derivable from one JSON event log, each with an estimator and an interval method selected by a Monte Carlo coverage study; (3) a gaming analysis pairing each inflation tactic with the check that exposes it; (4) four trust levels built from deployed tamper-evidence primitives; (5) a draft reporting standard. Simulation with known truth (1,000 replicates per cell, 6 scenarios, 3 sample sizes) selects defaults that hold 95 % coverage except where no session-level method can (robot-level clustering; hidden takeovers) and identifies the methods that fail (Poisson exact under over-dispersion: 0.72; exponential gap model: 0.18). Re-analysis of California DMV 2023-2024 disengagement reports (55 company-year-program groups) and ArmnetBench v0.1 (2,499 episodes, 7 policies, 8 tasks) shows that the statistics in use — miles per disengagement, pooled episode success — overstate precision by factors of 2-4 and reorder league tables once honest intervals are attached.

## 1. Prior art and the gap

These report intervention ratios as running averages without intervals; RoboCat and ALOHA report success rates without intervention logging ([Bousmalis2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11706) VERIFIED; [Zhao2023](https://doi.org/10.15607/RSS.2023.XIX.016) VERIFIED).

**Gap.** No source defines an observable control state per time step with rules for shared control, approvals, hints and supervision; none distinguishes local from remote humans in a required field except the DMV initiator column; none gives interval methods, clustering, censoring or unmeasured-time bounds; none addresses tamper evidence or gaming by the reporting party. See the gap table in [prior_art.md]({{artifact:40cbcdd3-0596-4291-99ef-c682a4596501}}).

## 2. Definitions

Normative text: [definitions.md]({{artifact:2168860d-f130-48a2-a36a-2e52c2e5c3a7}}); schema: [event.schema.json]({{artifact:efd8879d-429a-495f-81c9-4d3404f57a9b}}) (draft 2020-12, 10 event types; 11 embedded examples validate and 4 negative cases are rejected in the test suite).

Control is recorded per **scope** (default `whole_robot`; named body parts for humanoids). Each scope is in exactly one of seven states: `ai`, `human_local`, `human_remote`, `shared`, `safety_stop`, `idle`, `unknown`. A **transition** carries a reason in {`ai_requested_help`, `human_initiated`, `safety`, `planned`} and, for human states, an actor id. An **intervention** is a takeover (from `ai` into a human or shared state), an `approval` or a `hint`; planned transitions are human time but not interventions; `safety_stop` is neither AI nor human time. **Active time** excludes `idle` (after `idle_threshold_s`, default 30 s) and `unknown`. A **task** is a pre-registered unit of work bounded by `task_start`/`task_end` with a `task_definition_id` from a registry hashed at `session_start`.

Edge-case rulings: an approval click is one intervention adding `max(dwell, approval_min_dwell_s)` human time with a 5 s sensitivity variant always reported; a hint places the scope in `shared` for `hint_ttl_s` (60 s) unless declared as a discrete event; a reset between tasks is human time, excluded from task-level metrics only if pre-registered as `planned`; supervision without control is a session attribute (`supervision_ratio`), never a control state; with several humans the whole-robot state is human if any scope is human-controlled, with a scope-weighted share beside it; per-body-part control uses named scopes with declared weights. Unknown time yields two bounds (all unknown as human; all as AI) and an outer interval.

## 3. Metrics and estimators

Implementation: `autonomy_meter` v0.1.1 ([reference.zip]({{artifact:54785d39-c1b3-4a38-a036-bdf1833e2532}}); 44 tests pass, [pytest_output.txt]({{artifact:6f753fa2-d04a-45be-b835-f97bdc195789}})). Statistical primitives are package functions: `statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint` (Wilson, Clopper-Pearson; [docs](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.proportion.proportion_confint.html) VERIFIED), `scipy.stats.chi2` for the Garwood Poisson-exact interval (Garwood 1936, https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/28.3-4.437 CLAIM — formula checked numerically in tests), `statsmodels` NegativeBinomial (NB2) with log-exposure offset, GLM with `cov_type='cluster'`, `lifelines` KaplanMeierFitter / ExponentialFitter / WeibullFitter ([docs](https://lifelines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/fitters/univariate/KaplanMeierFitter.html) VERIFIED). Custom numerics are limited to the cluster-bootstrap loop, the Taylor-linearised ratio variance and the CUSUM recursion.

| metric | estimator | default interval (v0.1.1; chosen in Section 5) | secondary |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) time autonomy share `ai_s/active_s` | ratio of sums over sessions, any-human timeline | ratio-linearised t over clusters (`cluster_level` session or robot) | cluster bootstrap percentile (n >= 20); Wilson with effective n (single-session fallback); bounds and outer interval |
| (b) task autonomy rate | tasks with zero interventions / tasks | cluster-robust binomial GLM when >= 20 sessions; Wilson below | Clopper-Pearson; bounds |
| (c) interventions per active hour | pooled count / exposure | NB2 Wald, multi-start fit; Garwood only when the dispersion MLE is at zero | Garwood; cluster bootstrap; per-kind/per-reason; narrow DMV-like count |
| (d) gaps between interventions | Kaplan-Meier, last gap per session right-censored | KM median, exponential-Greenwood CI (descriptive) | Exponential and Weibull fits (`rho_` < 1 = bursty) |
| (e) human minutes per task | mean over tasks | cluster bootstrap-t with cluster-robust SE | percentile bootstrap; median |
| (f) breakdowns | (c) by reason/kind; (b)/(e) by task type | per-cell Garwood / Wilson | — |
| (g) drift | GLM log-link, offset log(hours), linear time; NB when dispersion > 1.5 | Wald on rate ratio per bin | tabular CUSUM (k = 0.5, h = 4) |
| (h) unmeasured | `unknown_s/observed_s`; bounds | — | conformance flag |

Test-length planning (`planning.py`): zero-event exposure `T = -ln(alpha)/lambda_max` for independent sessions (rule of three at alpha = 0.05; Hanley & Lippman-Hand 1983, https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/c607/ch08/zero_numerator.pdf VERIFIED excerpt); for a frailty shared by the whole test, `T = (alpha^(-a) - 1)/(a lambda_max)` with NB dispersion `a`; zero-failure tasks `ceil(ln alpha / ln(1 - p_max))` inflated by the Kish design effect `1 + (m-1) ICC`; exposure when events are expected via the one-sided Garwood/Clopper-Pearson bound; two-period power via a Wald test on the log rate ratio with variance `1/mu1 + 1/mu2 + 2a`.

## 4. Gaming and trust

**Gaming** ([gaming.md]({{artifact:6f17f197-1c45-443f-9341-185efd6e5384}})). Each inflation tactic is paired with the check that exposes it: task redefinition (task-duration distribution per `task_definition_id` vs prior period; registry hash); idle padding (`idle_share_of_observed` with cap and dual reporting); stopping instead of taking over (`safety_stops_per_active_hour`, stop dwell, `stop_dominated` flag); relabelling takeovers as assists (approval/hint counts per task; `with_approvals` variant with 5 s sensitivity); hidden remote operators (`human_remote` time, distinct remote actors; L3 disputed-time report; optional network witness); log editing (hash-chain continuity, signed checkpoints, contiguous session census); cherry-picking (mandatory exclusion list). Eight invariants run before any interval is printed. Three residual risks are disclosed, not measured: easy-deployment selection, control paths the logger cannot see, store-and-forward human planning.

**Trust** ([trust.md]({{artifact:1a6dad1c-3cc4-4a97-8fca-0d91307c57db}}); [trust_sources.csv]({{artifact:2fa8c16e-b13d-45d3-833e-478ebaa833e3}})). Append-only Merkle logs with inclusion and consistency proofs are specified by Certificate Transparency ([S02](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6962.txt) VERIFIED; [S03](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.txt) VERIFIED); Crosby & Wallach give tamper-evident-log semantics at about 1,750 signed events per second on one core ([S07](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/sec09/tech/full_papers/crosby.pdf) VERIFIED); hash-chained audit logs date to Schneier & Kelsey ([S06](https://doi.org/10.1145/317087.317089) CLAIM), Merkle trees and linked timestamping to Merkle and Haber & Stornetta ([S08](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-48184-2_32) CLAIM; [S09](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00196791) CLAIM). RFC 3161 time-stamp tokens bind a hash to a trustworthy time source ([S01](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161.txt) VERIFIED). Signed checkpoints and witness cosignatures in the C2SP formats provide the deployed split-view defence ([S15](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/C2SP/C2SP/main/tlog-checkpoint.md) VERIFIED; [S16](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/C2SP/C2SP/main/tlog-cosignature.md) VERIFIED; [S17](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/C2SP/C2SP/main/tlog-witness.md) VERIFIED; [S14](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/transparency-dev/witness/main/README.md) VERIFIED; [S19](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/trillian/master/README.md) VERIFIED; [S18](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/golang/proposal/master/design/25530-sumdb.md) VERIFIED); Sigstore Rekor is a public transparency log ([S12](https://doi.org/10.1145/3548606.3560596) CLAIM; [S13](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sigstore/rekor/main/README.md) VERIFIED). RFC 9334 defines attestation roles for hardware-rooted keys ([S04](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9334.txt) VERIFIED); TPM 2.0 is the reference hardware root ([S22](https://trustedcomputinggroup.org/resource/tpm-library-specification/) VERIFIED); NTP and PTP bound the clock offsets used in the two-witness tolerance ([S05](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5905.txt) VERIFIED; [S25](https://doi.org/10.1109/IEEESTD.2020.9120376) CLAIM).

Levels: **L1** self-reported; **L2** sealed log (per-event SHA-256 chain, C2SP checkpoints, RFC 3161 timestamps, publication recommended) closes post hoc editing; **L3** two witnesses (robot log and teleoperation-station log, distinct keys, cross-stamped, reconciled with tolerance `tau`, reporting `station_only_s`, `robot_only_s`, `disputed_s`) closes real-time mislabelling of remote control through the station; **L4** hardware-rooted keys with attestation plus an external witness. V1 ships L2 as mandatory and the L3 reconciler as software.

**Outside network witness.** RFC 9000 notes that encryption does not prevent timing and size from being used to correlate activity ([T01](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000.txt) VERIFIED); WebRTC and RTP carry real-time media with characteristic packet cadence ([T02](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8826.txt) VERIFIED; [T03](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3550.txt) VERIFIED). Analogue classification tasks reach high accuracy: website fingerprinting about 98 % closed-world ([T05](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02265) VERIFIED; [T06](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.06376) VERIFIED; [T04](https://doi.org/10.14722/ndss.2016.23477) CLAIM), encrypted video-stream identification with near-zero false positives from burst patterns ([T09](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-schuster.pdf) VERIFIED; [T10](https://doi.org/10.1145/3029806.3029821) CLAIM), smart-home device and activity inference — a camera's uplink rises by orders of magnitude when its live feed is watched ([T16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00955) VERIFIED; [T14](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmc.2018.2866249) CLAIM; [T15](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdcs.2017.283) CLAIM), cloud-gaming flow identification at 98.5 % ([T17](https://doi.org/10.3390/network1030015) VERIFIED; [T18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09786) VERIFIED; [T19](https://doi.org/10.1109/noms56928.2023.10154417) CLAIM), with reported drops to about 88 % under real-world drift ([T21](https://doi.org/10.1109/mcom.2019.1800819) CLAIM; [T22](https://doi.org/10.1109/tnsm.2019.2899085) CLAIM; [T23](https://doi.org/10.23919/tma58422.2023.10199052) CLAIM); remote-desktop activity detection and haptic traffic characterisation exist but do not measure teleoperation-session detection ([T24](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-32636-3_14) CLAIM; [T25](https://doi.org/10.1109/pimrc62392.2025.11275083) CLAIM; [T26](https://doi.org/10.1109/comst.2018.2851452) CLAIM). No primary study measures detection of robot teleoperation from a customer's network (UNKNOWN); trust.md 6.2 gives an explicit extrapolation (CLAIM) and lists failure cases: VPN multiplexing, store-and-forward hints, low-rate approval clicks, co-located LAN operators, traffic shaping ([T07](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2012.28) CLAIM; [T08](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-45744-4_2) CLAIM), vendor-cloud routing. The witness is advisory in v0.1.

## 5. Validation I: simulation with known truth

Record: [simulation README]({{artifact:f3efb3a7-649e-4f00-bc1f-123f1cdeb152}}); bundle [simulation_bundle.zip]({{artifact:fb1d92d6-d26c-4980-8a29-d518b1cb6f1f}}); tables [coverage_summary.csv]({{artifact:2d5c286a-418c-47b9-b9f4-c5c5d52fcfc0}}), [planning_zero_event_check.csv]({{artifact:f9abde9c-ff7d-4a83-9773-4efca64082e4}}).

**Design.** Session-level generator: log-normal active duration (median 1,200 s); intervention rate 8 per active hour times robot, day and session random effects (scenario iv), a Gamma frailty with a = 1 (ii), a Hawkes cluster process with branching ratio 0.6 (iii), or a log-linear halving over the period (v); three task types with rate multipliers 0.7/1.0/2.0 and failure probabilities 0.05/0.15/0.30; takeover dwell log-normal (median 40 s); 10 % unknown time as MCAR windows plus MNAR dropout starting 0.5 s before a takeover (vi). Truth is the superpopulation value from 40,000 sessions with robot and day effects redrawn (Monte Carlo SE of truth: share <= 0.0024, rate <= 0.18/h). 1,000 replicates per cell, 6 scenarios x n = 5, 20, 80 sessions, 19 metric-method rows, n_boot = 500; base seed 20261030. A 12-session JSONL rendering per scenario passed `validate_events` with zero errors and `full_report` recovered the generator truth within sampling error.

**Results (95 % nominal; Monte Carlo SE <= 0.016).**

1. Time share: ratio-linearised t over sessions covers 0.914-0.943 (Poisson) and 0.957 (NB, n = 80), with a floor of 0.867-0.868 at n = 5 under NB and Hawkes; percentile cluster bootstrap 0.809 (Poisson) and 0.786 (NB) at n = 5; Wilson with effective n over-covers (0.996-1.000) at 1.9-2.5 times the width and falls to 0.865 under clustering.
2. Interventions per hour: NB2 Wald 0.946-0.958 (Poisson), 0.889/0.935/0.954 at n = 5/20/80 (NB); Garwood 0.716 under NB dispersion ~3 and 0.834 under Hawkes bursts at n = 80, not improving with n.
3. Task autonomy rate: cluster-robust GLM 0.924/0.952 at n = 20/80 under NB vs Wilson 0.854/0.853; at n = 5 GLM 0.874 vs Wilson 0.952.
4. Human minutes per task: standard cluster bootstrap-t 0.925-0.943 (Poisson), 0.964 (NB, n = 80); percentile 0.803 at n = 5; the v0.1.0 bootstrap-t covered 0.830/0.854 because resampled duplicate sessions were merged into one cluster (fixed in v0.1.1).
5. Gap median: KM with exponential-Greenwood CI 0.949-0.952 (Poisson), 0.809 (NB), 0.885 (Hawkes); the exponential model collapses to 0.179 (NB) because its target ln2/rate = 0.1000 h differs from the population KM median 0.0768 h.
6. Robot/day clustering (4 robots): every session-level interval loses coverage as n grows (0.673-0.733 at n = 80); robot-level t intervals hold (0.953 share, 0.945 rate, 0.952 human minutes) at 1.5-3 times the width.
7. Drift: no method is harmed (relative bias of the period-average rate +0.002 at n = 80).
8. Informative dropout (10 % unknown, hidden takeovers): all sampling intervals cover the observed-process limit at nominal rates but miss the truth (share +3.7 %, rate -37 %, task rate +27 %, human minutes -58 %); only the outer bounds interval covers the share truth (1.000). Rate and human-minute metrics have no upper bound when events can be hidden.
9. Planning: at threshold rate 0.1/h with a frailty shared by the whole test, the rule of three yields a false "below threshold" claim 12.0 %, 15.7 % and 24.6 % of the time for a = 0.3, 0.5, 1.0 (nominal 5 %); `hours_for_zero_events_nb` gives 5.0 %, 4.9 %, 5.2 % (20,000 runs per cell); with independent 1-h sessions the rule of three is nearly right (5.2-5.8 %).

**Defaults adopted** (standard section 4.2): linearised t for (a) with the clustering unit at the level the claim generalises to; cluster-robust GLM at >= 20 clusters for (b); NB2 Wald for (c); KM median labelled descriptive for (d); bootstrap-t for (e); mandatory bounds and outer interval for (a)-(b); declared frailty structure for zero-event claims. Four defects in v0.1.0 (bootstrap-t clustering, exponential CI misuse, dispersion-switch rule, NB fit divergence on strongly over-dispersed groups — the last found on DMV data) were fixed in v0.1.1 with regression tests.

## 6. Validation II: California DMV disengagement reports, 2023-2024

Record: [dmv_analysis.md]({{artifact:cfa4ecc4-cdcc-4708-b42b-e7f79fccf375}}), [data-notes.md]({{artifact:85bd664e-b48d-4c0c-be30-6c398abd37f8}}), [rates_with_intervals.csv]({{artifact:55de15fd-6ee0-48e6-bcd9-927b50b184d0}}), [ranking_2024.csv]({{artifact:35356fde-8a14-4a5c-b7f8-30d5fb0628fd}}), [year_over_year.csv]({{artifact:fb926568-062f-41e2-aa71-3297c39ea896}}), [initiator_breakdown.csv]({{artifact:1de84af1-a4ff-4e6f-84ca-0b2076591975}}), raw files [dmv_raw_2023_2024.zip]({{artifact:abdc748c-613f-49d5-a7a1-dc39f6eb93f3}}), script [dmv_analysis.py]({{artifact:15917e59-e3ab-4bd2-abcc-ce0f5b36283e}}).

**Data.** Twelve official CSVs (disengagement and mileage; standard, driverless and first-time-filer variants; reporting years 2023 and 2024) retrieved from dmv.ca.gov on 2026-10-30 ([DMV-CSV-2023](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/2023-autonomous-vehicle-disengagement-reports-csv/) VERIFIED; [DMV-CSV-2024](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/file/2024-autonomous-vehicle-disengagement-reports-csv/) VERIFIED). The 2025-period files are no longer published after the DMV's 2026 site restructure; only the press release remains ([DMV-NEWS-2026](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/news-and-media/autonomous-vehicle-permit-holders-in-california-logged-more-than-9-million-test-miles-between-december-1-2024-and-november-30-2025/) VERIFIED) — UNKNOWN for 2025. 44 raw manufacturer strings map to 26 companies (`manufacturer_name_map.csv`); company-level counts from the disengagement files equal the mileage-file annual totals for all 55 company-year-program groups; mileage totals reconcile to the DMV press releases (2023 driverless 3,267,792 mi exact; 2024 driverless 552,896 vs 552,895 stated; safety-driver totals within 0.05 %). Data-quality findings: 10 VIN-level count mismatches (Motional, Vueron, WeRide), lower-case VINs (Ghost), masked VINs (Zoox). Reproducibility: rerunning `dmv_analysis.py` on the raw CSVs in this session regenerated all six result tables with a maximum numeric difference of 0.0 in the rate, interval, ranking and initiator tables (1.5e-7 in year_over_year.csv); the only difference from the first run is the diagnostic column `n_vins_in_dis_not_in_mileage` for Ghost Autonomy 2023 (9 before, 0 after the script's VIN-case normalisation), which enters no reported statistic. The bundled `company_year.csv` and `vin_year.csv` are the script-generated versions.

**Mapping.** A disengagement is a `human_initiated`/`safety` takeover or an AV-initiated `ai_requested_help` transition with distance exposure; the files test metric (c) in distance units and its intervals, not time share or human minutes. The definition is safety-scoped and leaves the "requires" judgement to the filer ([DMV-REG-2019](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/uploads/2020/06/Adopted-Regulatory-Text-2019.pdf) VERIFIED).

![2024 forest plot: disengagements per 1,000 autonomous miles with Garwood, NB and bootstrap intervals]({{artifact:375763d2-a361-448e-9964-4ff5f442eba4}})

**Results.** 15 of 55 groups have zero disengagements and no miles-per-disengagement value; their rule-of-three upper bounds span six orders of magnitude with exposure. Of 34 groups with >= 2 VINs and >= 1 event, 17 exceed dispersion index 1.5; where NB is identified (21 groups) its interval is a median 1.91 times the Garwood width (Apple 2023 4.7x, Motional 2023 5.5x, Beep 24x). Waymo 2024 testing permit: 244 events / 2.39 million mi = 0.102 per 1,000 mi, Garwood [0.090, 0.116], NB [0.086, 0.135]; Waymo 2024 driverless: 22 events / 516,572 mi, Garwood [0.0267, 0.0645], NB [0.041, 0.132] (dispersion driven by 22 events over 789 mostly-zero VINs), bootstrap [0.0252, 0.0652]. Year over year, Waymo's testing-permit rate rose 1.77x (NB-SE CI [1.35, 2.32]) while testing miles fell from 3.67 to 2.39 million; Ghost Autonomy's apparent 1.36x rise is not distinguishable from no change once clustering is included.

![Naive miles-per-disengagement rank vs upper-bound rank, 2024]({{artifact:b771270c-4ac4-4d20-9bff-6297f3bfa02f}})

**Where comparisons are unreliable.** Four companies tie for "best" by naive MPI with zero events, three having driven 3, 40 and 513 miles; ranking by the widest 95 % upper bound moves Apollo from rank 4 to 20, Vueron 3 to 16, Nullmax 2 to 9, Waymo 9 to 2, Zoox 5 to 1. Among 20 companies, 80 of 190 pairwise Garwood intervals overlap and 96 of 190 under the widest honest interval; 17 of 19 adjacent ranks overlap. Seven of 20 2024 testing-permit companies drove under 1,000 miles (Bosch: 77 events in 164 miles, 469 per 1,000 mi). First-time filers May Mobility (20,694 events, 13,660 mi) and Beep (9,159 events, 17,066 mi; per-VIN dispersion index 264) report low-speed shuttle operations over a 23-month window and exceed the whole 2024 testing fleet (1,773 events) by an order of magnitude; they are excluded from pooled statistics. Deployment-permit miles are exempt ([DMV-NEWS-2026](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/news-and-media/autonomous-vehicle-permit-holders-in-california-logged-more-than-9-million-test-miles-between-december-1-2024-and-november-30-2025/) VERIFIED), so each company's reported slice is selected. Driverless-permit "disengagements" are remote retrievals, not takeovers. The DMV's comparability disclaimer ([DMV-NEWS-2026](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/news-and-media/autonomous-vehicle-permit-holders-in-california-logged-more-than-9-million-test-miles-between-december-1-2024-and-november-30-2025/) VERIFIED) is supported quantitatively: intervals expose the within-company sampling floor but cannot repair between-company differences in definition and operational domain.

**What this validates.** Rates with intervals, never inverse rates (standard 4.7); mandatory clustering unit and number of clusters, and both model-based and resampling intervals when they differ by more than 2x (standard 4.2); the NB Wald failure regimes (fewer than ~3 clusters; a handful of events in one cluster) are named; VIN-month clustering raised the dispersion index relative to per-VIN in 20 of 34 groups, supporting a session unit no coarser than a day (standard 1.7).

## 7. Validation III: ArmnetBench v0.1 (SO-101)

Record: [armnetbench_analysis.md]({{artifact:6bf591df-a411-4aeb-8355-30ee8daa0923}}), [policy_pooled_intervals.csv]({{artifact:2f375041-bf9a-45ca-88ac-af04e3fd24b3}}), [success_by_policy_task.csv]({{artifact:a4c516e9-e0f5-4d67-a479-b28ecea105e9}}), [duration_by_task.csv]({{artifact:5728f85d-9835-4aa7-989f-35a043c316ab}}), [planning_policy_level.csv]({{artifact:e649e594-64c1-4655-b268-99b404a88bb9}}), episode table [armnetbench_episodes.csv]({{artifact:2f2e6846-3cbd-40d0-bb71-14a08b8627a0}}), script [armnetbench_analysis.py]({{artifact:e5380fbf-5dec-4a8a-9fa0-c124d772def9}}).

**Data.** Hugging Face dataset `armnet/armnetbench_v01_lerobot_so101` (LeRobot v3.0; Apache-2.0), retrieved 2026-10-30 ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/armnet/armnetbench_v01_lerobot_so101) VERIFIED): 2,499 episodes, 8 tasks, 7 policies (ACT, Diffusion, SmolVLA, pi0, pi0.5, GR00T N1.7, MolmoAct 2) plus 400 teleoperated reference episodes (50 per task), labels successful/failure/suboptimal = 915/1,532/52; counts verified against the `meta/episodes` parquet. The dataset has no intervention events — each episode is wholly autonomous or wholly teleoperated — so the measurable quantities are task autonomy rate conditioned on success, human minutes per task (teleop demo duration) and autonomous vs teleop duration; interventions per hour and within-episode time share cannot be measured.

![Strict success rate by policy and task]({{artifact:e99b1f0b-07a5-4575-a96e-e439cc91e615}})

**Results.** Pooled strict success per policy (n, rate, Wilson 95 %, task-clustered 95 %): pi0.5 240, 0.454, [0.392, 0.517], [0.272, 0.636]; pi0 239, 0.364, [0.306, 0.427], [0.176, 0.552]; GR00T N1.7 240, 0.346, [0.289, 0.408], [0.194, 0.498]; Diffusion 240, 0.221, [0.173, 0.277], [0.068, 0.374]; ACT 330, 0.203, [0.163, 0.250], [0.029, 0.377]; MolmoAct 2 480, 0.148, [0.119, 0.182], [0.065, 0.231]; SmolVLA 330, 0.136, [0.103, 0.178], [0.033, 0.240]. Between-task ICCs of 0.10-0.39 give design effects of 6-16, so task-clustered intervals are 2.4-4.0 times wider than Wilson; cluster-robust GLM and cluster bootstrap over tasks agree closely. Cable clip had zero autonomous successes in 270 episodes. Under independent-episode analysis pi0.5 beats pi0, GR00T N1.7 and Diffusion; under the paired task bootstrap only pi0.5 vs pi0 remains separated from a rate ratio of 1 ([1.03, 1.66]). Counting suboptimal completions as successes moves pooled rates by at most 0.050 and swaps two adjacent ranks inside overlapping intervals. MolmoAct 2's pooled rate depends on which checkpoints are included (0.196 card-linked vs 0.100 older), so the standard requires the evaluated model version per episode.

![Pooled per-policy success with naive and task-clustered intervals]({{artifact:ea99d720-ebd1-425f-8d25-6d3bf27b8af5}})

Durations: teleop episodes average 15.1 s pooled (task-cluster bootstrap [11.5, 19.6]) = 0.25 human minutes per task; successful autonomous episodes take 0.86-1.14 times the teleop duration of the same task; failed episodes average 27.7 s with an UNKNOWN stopping rule, so robot time per failure is a lower bound and human reset time is off-record. Planning: no policy can establish "pooled success > 50 %" at any sample size (none exceeds 50 %); with task clustering, "success > 25 %" needs about 137 episodes for pi0.5 and 516 for pi0, versus 17 and 56 if episodes were independent — adding tasks rather than episodes per task is the efficient route.

![Episode duration: teleop vs successful autonomous vs failed]({{artifact:c9f50297-29f8-40fc-9e0f-6b3c6be6e7e7}})

**What this validates.** The task-clustered default for metric (b) (standard 4.2); design-effect inflation in test-length planning (standard 4.6); recording the evaluated model version and success criterion per episode; and that "human minutes per task = 0" for policy episodes is not an autonomy claim because the human time (resets, stops) moved off the record — the unmeasured-time rule (standard 4.3) exists for this case.
